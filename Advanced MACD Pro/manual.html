1. Introduction & Philosophy

1.1 What Advanced MACD Pro is

Advanced MACD Pro lives in its own oscillator pane under the price chart. It plots a MACD line, a signal line, a zero reference line and a histogram — the familiar four — and then does four things that the classic indicator does not do:

  • It adapts. Every period and window is derived from the resolved bar duration through an explicit scaling law (§3). There is no lookup table of “M1 values” and “H4 values”: one formula, one anchor (H1), one curve. Move the indicator to another timeframe and the whole parameter set re-derives itself, including the divergence geometry, the auxiliary indicator periods, the volatility baseline and the drawing budgets.
  • It reads divergences properly. Pivots are prominence-filtered fractals that publish only after their right-hand leg has closed; every admissible pairing inside the distance window is scored and the strongest one wins (not just “compare with the previous pivot”); regular and hidden families are graded A/B/C, confirmed against RSI and the histogram, pushed through eight hard gates, and given a 0–100 quality score built from thirteen weighted components (§6).
  • It listens to higher timeframes. A confluence ladder of up to three higher timeframes is built, and each level gets its own fully adaptive profile — the ladder is not the primary setting re-read on a coarser series, it is what the indicator would compute if you had opened that chart yourself (§9).
  • It measures itself. Every registered divergence becomes a record with an entry price taken at the confirmation bar (never at the pivot, which would bake in look-ahead), an ATR-denominated target and stop, and a horizon. Outcomes resolve incrementally on closed bars only, are bucketed per signal family, and the measured win rate is fed back as one of the thirteen quality components. The counters persist in the platform's LocalStorage per symbol and profile (§7).

1.2 The philosophy in one paragraph

Adapt, don't look up. Measure, don't promise. Never repaint a confirmed signal. A parameter set is a hypothesis about bar duration; the scaling law states that hypothesis once, in a formula you can read (§3.3), instead of hiding it in a table nobody can audit. A quality score is a weighted mean of normalised, scale-free components — not a probability, and never presented as one. A win rate is printed with its sample size, or the word collecting, or the word off. And a signal that has been confirmed cannot move again: pivots publish after their right leg closes, outcomes latch, marker anchors are computed from bar open times rather than from “now”, and a history reload rebuilds everything from scratch instead of patching stale indices.

2. Architecture & Guarantees

2.1 Two index spaces, and why that matters

The indicator can run on a source timeframe that is not the chart timeframe (Source Timeframe). That creates two index spaces, and confusing them is the classic source of multi-timeframe bugs:

SpaceIndexed byWhat lives there
Source spacebars of the resolved source timeframe (_sourceBars)Every internal series: price input, MACD, signal, histogram, scale, cross latch, pivots, divergences, outcomes, learning, alerts. All of it lives in growable SeriesBuffer arrays, not in the platform's output series
Chart spacebars of the chart you are looking at (Bars)The 11 output series and every chart object's X coordinate

Writing a source index into a chart-indexed IndicatorDataSeries is unsafe whenever the source is finer than the chart (more source bars than chart bars) — it silently writes to the wrong bar or out of range. This indicator therefore keeps the mathematics in its own buffers and only ever writes final, chart-indexed values to the outputs, through the mapping functions of §2.3.

2.2 The computation pipeline, in the exact order it runs

Inside the MACD pipeline, one call to ComputeRange(from, to) performs, in this order:

  1. Price input — build the oscillator's source series for the range (close / typical / weighted / median / Heikin-Ashi / volume-price-adjusted, §5.2). Missing components fall back to the close so one bad bar cannot blank the oscillator for a whole window.
  2. Efficiency ratio — raw Kaufman ER per bar, then smoothed by an EMA over 2 × ER Period bars. This is the regime weight.
  3. MACD line — either one fast/slow pair, or (with the filter bank on) the weighted blend of a responsive and a smooth pair. The blend is defined only when both legs are defined.
  4. |MACD| and its rolling mean — Scale, a simple MA over NormWindow = clamp(4×Slow, 40..600) bars. This is the oscillator's own yardstick.
  5. Signal line — a moving average of the MACD line, over the resolved signal period, with the kernel chosen by Signal Line MA Type.
  6. Histogram = MACD − signal; then |histogram| and its rolling mean (HistScale), which is the unit the early-entry band and the “STRONG/NORMAL/WEAK” panel row are measured in.
  7. Cross latch — the sequential ±1 state machine behind every crossover signal (§5.6, §8).

Then, once per chart bar, Calculate(index) runs the outer pipeline:

  1. reload / shrink detection → full rebuild if history changed shape (§2.6);
  2. Sync(true) on the primary pipeline: append new source bars, re-evaluate the forming one;
  3. rewrite the just-closed chart bar with the strict mapping (§2.3);
  4. map this chart bar onto the source series and write the outputs;
  5. crossover markers (§8);
  6. pivots & divergences (§6) — deliberately before the confluence / volatility / outcome updates, because the pivot scanner advances those three states itself to their canonical per-confirmation point, which is what makes a bulk history pass bitwise identical to the live pass;
  7. MTF confluence (§9), volatility baseline, outcome tracking (§7);
  8. on the last chart bar only: alerts (§11), panel refresh (§10), and arming of the alert latches at the end of the initial history pass.

2.6 History reloads, symbol switches, reconnects

The indicator subscribes to the source series' Reloaded and HistoryLoaded events (once per series, de-duplicated by reference, because a confluence level can legitimately point at the very same series as the primary source) and additionally compares the source bar count on every Calculate. Either trigger sets a rebuild flag, and the next pass runs RebuildAfterHistoryChange:

  1. Re-derive the timeframe geometry (bar spans), because everything below maps indices through it.
  2. Invalidate and re-sync the primary pipeline; invalidate and re-sync every confluence level.
  3. Clear pivots, divergences (removing their chart objects first), cross markers, the volatility model, the deduplication slots and every alert latch — including the confluence sign latch and any deferred alert.
  4. Reset the learning counters. They are a statistic of the records that were just discarded; keeping them would count the same market events again on every reload and inflate the sample counts.
  5. Rewrite the whole output series (RecomputeAllOutputs), because historical chart bars are never visited again by Calculate — merely clearing them would strand every marker older than the live edge.
  6. Re-arm the history pass, so a crossover that already happened while loading cannot fire an alert afterwards.
Tip If your terminal reconnects often, keep Auto Load History = true: the indicator requests more bars in a bounded loop (at most 12 attempts, stopping as soon as a request returns nothing) until the resolved profile's warm-up requirement is met, so a deep reload does not leave you with a half-computed oscillator.

3. Timeframe Resolution & the Adaptive Engine

3.1 How the source timeframe is resolved

Source Timeframe offers CurrentChart, twelve fixed timeframes (M1, M5, M15, M30, H1, H2, H4, H6, H12, D1, W1, MN) and Custom. Every name is resolved through the platform's own parser (TimeFrame.Parse) rather than a hard-coded table, so the set of usable timeframes can never drift from what the running build of cTrader actually supports.

Custom Timeframe Name accepts, in this order of attempts:

  1. a canonical platform name — Minute15, Hour4, Renko10, Range50, Tick1000…
  2. trader shorthand — M1…M10080, H1…H24, D1…D7, T1…T1000, W/W1, MN/MN1/MO, RENKOn, RANGEn;
  3. Heikin-Ashi variants of any of the above — HeikinH4, HeikinAshiMinute15…;
  4. the literal words current / chart, which mean “use the chart”.

Case, spaces and punctuation are ignored. A name the platform rejects falls back to the chart timeframe and is reported: WARNING: Custom Timeframe '…' is not a timeframe this platform recognises, with the list of valid forms. With Debug Log on, the exact reason the parser refused is printed too — useful for telling “my spelling was wrong” from “this build has no such timeframe”.

3.2 How the bar duration is measured

The scaling law needs the bar duration in minutes. TimeFrame.Size is internal to the platform API, so the duration is derived from the timeframe's public name, with an empirical fallback:

3.3 The scaling law

One formula, no lookup tables. With D = source bar duration in minutes, Dref = 60 (H1), ε = Microstructure Noise Horizon (min), δ = the horizon exponent of Timeframe Scaling:

N(D) = N_ref * ( (1 + eps/D) / (1 + eps/D_ref) ) ^ (gamma/2) * ( D_ref / D ) ^ delta gamma = 1 (fixed; the exponent applied is gamma/2 = 0.5) delta = 0.00 Timeframe Scaling = BarCount (constant bar count) delta = 0.15 Timeframe Scaling = Balanced (default: a mild tilt toward longer periods on fast charts) delta = 1.00 Timeframe Scaling = TimeHorizon (constant wall-clock horizon) scale is then clamped to [floor, Max Period Multiplier]: floor = 1.00 High Timeframe Policy = Canonical (never shrink below the reference set at/above H1) floor = 0.35 High Timeframe Policy = HorizonMatched (allow shrinkage, but not below a reliability floor)

The two terms do different jobs. The noise term keeps the statistical reliability of the smoothed difference constant: per-bar variance is σ²·D + σ²eps, so the number of bars that must be averaged to reach a fixed signal-to-noise ratio grows as sqrt(1 + eps/D). The horizon term moves the indicator between “constant bar count” (δ = 0) and “constant wall-clock horizon” (δ = 1). The default δ = 0.15 exists because the noise term alone under-corrects in practice on fast charts.

A period is then scaled with a per-parameter exponent, because not every parameter is horizon-driven: ScaleBars(ref, scale, exponent, min, max, gain) computes round(ref × scaleexponent × gain) and clamps to [min, max]. The gain is 1 / Global Sensitivity, which is why sensitivity divides periods: 2.0 gives half the periods (twice as fast), 0.5 doubles them, and the label agrees with the effect on every timeframe.

5. The MACD Core

5.1 Structure

The core is a single self-contained class instantiated once for the source timeframe and once per confluence level, so the indicator behaves identically on every timeframe — each instance with its own automatically derived profile. Every kernel in it is a strictly causal recursion: the value at index i depends only on inputs at ≤ i and on already-computed outputs at < i. That property is what makes an exact incremental update possible, so re-evaluating the forming bar on every tick reproduces exactly what a full recomputation would produce — no drift, no O(history) rescan.

The volume-price-adjusted input, step by step:

per bar i: sigma_i = (H_i - L_i) / C_i relative volatility body_i = |C_i - O_i| / (H_i - L_i) directional conviction of the candle (both averaged over max(3, VP Window) bars -> sigmaBar_i, bodyBar_i) weight_k = volume_k * clamp(sigma_k / sigmaBar_i, 0.25, 4) * clamp(body_k / bodyBar_i, 0.25, 4) price_i = SUM(weight_k * C_k) / SUM(weight_k) over k in [i - VP Window + 1, i]

This is the scale-preserving form of the paper's idea: σ and the body ratio are normalised against their own rolling means and used as conviction weights, not as multiplicative price factors. The adjusted series therefore stays in price units and the resulting MACD remains directly comparable to the classic one — which matters, because every threshold in the divergence engine is denominated in ATR or in the oscillator's own RMS. Robustness details: a bar with no usable price contributes nothing rather than poisoning the average; volume that is missing, zero or negative is folded onto the neutral 1.0; and the whole scan is memoised on the forming bar's inputs, so a stream of ticks that change nothing costs O(1).

Cost VolumePriceAdjusted is the heaviest input: its window scan cannot be reduced to O(1) per tick, because both conviction factors are ratios against rolling means that move with the forming bar. On a deep history the difference is measurable; on a live chart the memo absorbs most of it. The panel's Input row always tells you which one is active.

Two implementation details worth knowing, because they are the difference between an indicator that survives a bad bar and one that goes blank:

  • Missing components are substituted, not propagated. 5.5 The regime-adaptive filter bank

With Regime-Adaptive Filter Bank = true (default) and a non-zero Filter Bank Spread (default 0.35), the MACD line is not one oscillator but a bank of two, mixed by the market's own regime:

responsive legs = Fast * (1 - spread) , Slow * (1 - spread) (default: 8 / 17) smooth legs = Fast * (1 + spread) , Slow * (1 + spread) (default: 16 / 35) w_i = smoothed Kaufman Efficiency Ratio, clamped to [0, 1] (0.5 while it warms up) MACD_i = w_i * MACD_responsive_i + (1 - w_i) * MACD_smooth_i

w → 1 means an efficient, trending regime, where the responsive leg should dominate; w → 0 means chop, where the smooth leg suppresses the whipsaw. The raw ER is far too twitchy to drive this on its own, which is why it is smoothed over 2 × ER Period bars (a derived quantity, §3.5).

Warm-up semantics The blend is defined only once both legs are defined. Substituting whichever leg warmed up first would inject a short stretch of values from a different filter into the normalisation window, into the early-entry band and into the crossover latch — a scale discontinuity exactly where a first crossover is most likely to be fabricated. The cost is a slightly later start (about spread × Slow bars, ~18 at the defaults), which the warm-up warning already accounts for because it counts the deeper leg.

The confluence ladder runs with the bank off at every level, by design: the ladder reports direction, not responsiveness, and a regime-mixed score would be hard to compare across timeframes.

5.6 Signal Sensitivity Lambda and the crossover latch

Signal Sensitivity Lambda ∈ [0.80, 1.00] controls how early a crossover is announced. At λ = 1 (default) the band collapses to zero and the latch reproduces the classic crossing exactly. Below 1, the trigger is advanced to the bar on which the histogram climbs out of the band on its way to a crossing:

band_i = (1 - lambda) * HistUnit_i HistUnit_i = rolling mean |histogram| over NormWindow bars, or - while that mean is still warming up - the mean |histogram| over whatever bars DO exist (it converges to the rolling mean exactly)

The unit matters. Measuring the band in the oscillator's own mean absolute histogram is what makes λ mean the same thing on M1 and on W1, on EURUSD and on Bitcoin: λ = 0.92 always means “trigger once the histogram has closed 8% of its typical distance to zero”. This is also why λ is deliberately not part of the adaptive profile — scaling it on top of an already scale-free quantity would adapt it twice.

Two rules keep the advance honest:

  1. Direction. An early trigger may only fire in the direction the histogram is leaving, never the one it currently occupies — otherwise the latch would flip back on the next bar and emit BUY/BUY or SELL/SELL pairs out of pure noise inside the band.
  2. Consumption. An early trigger is consumed by the crossing it anticipated, so the crossing itself does not emit a second one.

The result is exactly one trigger per oscillator excursion. That cannot be guaranteed by a local two-bar test once the band is wider than zero: the bullish band edge (−band) then sits below the bearish one (+band), so the two conditions overlap and a difference that dwells inside the band and then crosses zero satisfies neither. The latch is a strict causal recursion, so re-evaluating the forming bar reproduces exactly what a full recomputation would produce.

Three further details of the latch, each of which prevents a specific class of phantom signal:

  • Numerical noise floor. Differences within 1e-12 × |price| are snapped to exact zero. On a dead-flat tape the two MA chains accumulate independent rounding error and the histogram jitters around zero by a few ulps; without the floor, strict inequalities would fabricate crossovers — phantom markers and alerts on a completely flat market. The floor is derived from the price scale, never from an absolute constant, so it behaves identically on an instrument quoted at 1e-6 and one quoted at 1e6. It deliberately has no absolute component: an absolute 1e-9 floor is larger than the entire histogram of any instrument quoted below ~1e-3, and would silence the indicator permanently on crypto crosses, spread and rate instruments.
  • Hysteresis at exactly zero. A bar that lands on zero inherits the previous side, so a flat tape cannot fabricate crossings.
  • First bar with a histogram adopts the side the oscillator is already on and emits no trigger: there is no previous state to have moved away from.

6. The Divergence Engine

6.1 The path from a bar to a drawn signal

  1. Confirm a pivot. Every source bar that has just become confirmable is tested for a significant fractal pivot (§6.2). “Confirmable” means its right-hand window consists only of closed bars.
  2. Search the pool. The new pivot is paired against every older pivot of the same side inside [MinPivotDistance, MaxPivotDistance], up to Max Pivot Candidates pairings, and each pairing is scored. The strongest one wins — this is the single largest accuracy improvement available to a pivot-based detector, because divergences that span an intermediate pivot are no longer missed (§6.3).
  3. Classify and grade. Regular or hidden, bullish or bearish, A/B/C, with the zero-line rule and the two confirmations applied on the way (§6.4–6.5).
  4. Run the hard gates at the confirmation bar (§6.6). A rejection is logged with its reason when Debug Log is on.
  5. Score the quality — thirteen weighted components, 0–100 (§6.7). Below Min Quality Score the candidate is dropped and the drop is logged.
  6. Register an immutable record, claim the de-duplication slot, draw it if it is inside the drawing window, place the trigger marker at the confirmation bar, mark the panel dirty and raise the alert if the quality clears the alert bar (§6.8).
  7. Track the outcome on closed bars only, and feed the family's win rate back into future quality scores (§7).
Canonical state advance — why the scan order matters Divergences run before the confluence, volatility and outcome updates in the per-bar pipeline, because the pivot scanner advances those three states itself, to the exact point they would have been at when that pivot confirmed. Live, a pivot at bar c registers on the first tick of source bar c + lookback + 1, at which moment confluence reflects bar c + lookback + 1, the volatility model has consumed bar c + lookback and the outcomes have resolved up to c + lookback − 1. A bulk history pass confirms several pivots in one call, so without this per-confirmation advance every registration would read end-of-batch state instead — and quality scores, therefore which divergences pass at all, would differ between the live pass and any recompute. With it, the two are identical. The calls are watermark-guarded, so the total cost is unchanged.

6.3 Multi-candidate search and the pairing score

The pool is walked from the newest pivot backwards. A pairing is skipped when the distance is below MinPivotDistance; the walk stops when it exceeds MaxPivotDistance; at most Max Pivot Candidates pairings are evaluated. Every surviving pairing is assessed, and the one with the highest Score is taken forward. The score is a weighted sum, deliberately not normalised (it only ever compares candidates against each other):

Score = 2.0 * min(oscLegRms, 1.5) / 1.5 oscillator disagreement - the core of the pattern + 1.5 * min(priceLegAtr, 5.0) / 5.0 size of the price leg + 1.0 * min(mean prominence, 2.0) / 2.0 how well formed BOTH pivots are + 1.0 * DistanceFit(distance) separation membership (below) + 0.8 * RsiStrength (0.4 if RSI unknown) graded RSI agreement + 0.6 / 0.0 / 0.3 histogram agrees / disagrees / unknown + 0.7 / 0.45 / 0.2 grade A / B / C

DistanceFit is a trapezoidal membership function of the pivot separation — very tight pairs are usually one swing split in two, very wide pairs lose causal connection:

low = MinPivotDistance + 0.10 * (MaxPivotDistance - MinPivotDistance) high = MinPivotDistance + 0.60 * (MaxPivotDistance - MinPivotDistance) d <= low -> 0.45 + 0.55 * (d - Min) / (low - Min) (rises from 0.45 at the minimum) low < d <= high -> 1.0 (the plateau) d > high -> clamp(1 - (d - high) / (Max - high), 0.20, 1.0)

The confirmation bar of a candidate is min(pivot + PivotLookback, lastSourceBar) — the first bar on which the pivot could have been known. Everything below (gates, quality, entry price, alerts) is evaluated at that bar, never at the pivot.

6.4 Regular, hidden, and the A/B/C grade

A low pivot always produces a bullish signal and a high pivot a bearish one — for regular (reversal) and hidden (continuation) families alike. What differs is which leg moved against which:

PivotPriceOscillatorClassMeaning
Highhigher highlower oscillator highRegular bearishthe rally is not being confirmed by momentum — exhaustion
Highlower highhigher oscillator highHidden bearishmomentum is stronger than the shallow price pullback suggests — continuation down
Lowlower lowhigher oscillator lowRegular bullishthe sell-off is not being confirmed by momentum — exhaustion
Lowhigher lowlower oscillator lowHidden bullishmomentum is weaker than the shallow bounce suggests — continuation up

Divergence Kinds selects which families are allowed (RegularOnly, HiddenOnly, both by default).

The zero-line rule (Appel / Brown): with Require Zero-Line Match on, a regular bearish divergence must be built entirely above zero and a regular bullish one entirely below zero. Hidden divergences are exempt — the rule is about exhaustion at an extreme, and a continuation pattern is not one. The depth inside the correct zone is measured as ZoneDepth (in units of the oscillator's own scale, clamped to 0…3) and contributes to the quality score.

The grade separates genuine momentum exhaustion from a shallow diagonal:

toward = family-aware oscillator delta, normalised by the oscillator's scale: regular : high pivot -> (q - p), low pivot -> (p - q) (the fade) hidden : high pivot -> (p - q), low pivot -> (q - p) (the push) crossedZero = the two pivots sit on opposite sides of the zero line Grade A if crossedZero or toward >= 0.60 Grade B if toward >= 0.30 Grade C otherwise
Why the delta is family-aware Measuring hidden divergences with the regular-direction delta gives them a negative “toward” by construction, so no hidden divergence could ever reach the A or B thresholds and every one would be silently graded C. Class A means the oscillator not only disagrees but has turned back through zero — exhaustion, not geometry.

Follow-through measures the retraced fraction, not the distance from the pivot FollowThrough = (close − lowest low) / (highest high − lowest low) over [pivot, confirmation] for a bullish signal (mirrored for bearish), clamped to 0…1. A confirmed pivot is by construction the extreme of its window, so “is the close above the pivot low?” is always true and would filter nothing at all. 0 means price is still sitting at the extreme, 1 means it has retraced the whole post-pivot range. The measure is scale-free, so one threshold works on M1 and on D1. When it cannot be measured (no range, missing bar) it returns the neutral 0.5 rather than guessing.

6.7 The quality score

A weighted, bounded 0–100 score. Every component is normalised against a scale-free reference (ATR for price, the rolling mean absolute MACD for the oscillator), so the score means the same thing on EURUSD M1 and on XAUUSD Weekly. Components that cannot be evaluated are dropped from both the numerator and the denominator rather than being replaced by a neutral value, so an unavailable input can never inflate or deflate the result: quality = 100 × Σ(w·v) / Σw over the available components.

7. Outcomes & Adaptive Learning

7.1 What is measured

Every registered divergence becomes a measured trade hypothesis, denominated in ATR so that one set of numbers means the same thing on every instrument and timeframe:

entry = close of the CONFIRMATION bar (pivot + lookback) - never the pivot bar entryAtr = ATR at the confirmation bar (mean true range fallback while ATR warms up) target = Outcome Target (x ATR) default 1.0 stop = Outcome Stop (x ATR) default 1.5 horizon = Outcome Horizon (resolved, wall-clock capped at 120 days in automatic mode) per closed bar k in (confirmation, confirmation + horizon]: favourable = bullish ? (high_k - entry)/entryAtr : (entry - low_k)/entryAtr adverse = bullish ? (entry - low_k)/entryAtr : (high_k - entry)/entryAtr BestMove = max(BestMove, favourable); WorstMove = max(WorstMove, adverse) WIN (+1) when BestMove >= target and BestMove >= WorstMove -> stop scanning LOSS (-1) when WorstMove >= stop and BestMove < target -> stop scanning EXPIRED (+2) when the horizon elapses with neither touched PENDING (0) until one of the three happens
  • Intrabar extremes, not closes. A target touched by a wick counts as touched, because that is what a real limit order would have done.
  • Closed bars only. The scan runs to currentSourceIndex − 1; the forming bar is never evidence.
  • Latched and incremental. Each record remembers ScannedTo, so the total cost over the whole history is O(bars) instead of O(bars × horizon), and a later wick can never un-resolve an outcome.
  • Target wins ties. When a single bar touches both levels, the favourable one is credited if it is at least as large — the optimistic reading, stated here rather than buried.
  • Unmeasurable records are marked expired (no ATR, no entry price) rather than being left pending forever or being counted as a win.
  • The scan is skipped entirely unless a new source bar has closed since the last one, so a chart finer than the source pays nothing per tick.

7.2 The four buckets

Outcomes are accumulated per signal family, because a regular bullish divergence and a hidden bearish one are not the same hypothesis and should not share a track record:

BucketPanel namePopulation
0Reg Bullregular bullish

Each bucket stores wins, losses and expired counts; the win rate is wins / (wins + losses) — expired signals are neither wins nor losses, they are signals that did not resolve, and folding them into either side would invent a result.

7.3 How it feeds back

When Adaptive Learning is on and a bucket has at least Min Learning Samples resolved outcomes, that bucket's win rate enters the quality score as component 13 with weight 0.08 (§6.7). Below that sample count the component is dropped from numerator and denominator, not replaced by 0.5: a track record of two trades is not evidence, and treating it as neutral-but-counted would let a lucky pair drag every subsequent score.

This is a feedback loop, and it is deliberately a weak one Weight 0.08 out of a total of ~1.10 means a family with a 0% win rate loses about 6 quality points and a family with a 100% win rate gains about 2 relative to the neutral case. Enough to tilt selection toward what has been working on your feed, not enough to let a small sample take over the detector.

8. Crossover Signals & Markers

8.1 What a crossover signal is

A trigger is the transition of the latch described in §5.6: +1 on a bullish flip, −1 on a bearish one, read from the sequential state machine rather than from a two-bar comparison. DetectSignalCross(s) answers whether the latch flipped at source bar s. With λ = 1 this is exactly the classic MACD/signal crossing; below 1 it is the advanced trigger.

A separate detector watches the zero line (DetectZeroCross), with the same noise floor and the same hysteresis at exactly zero, so a flat tape cannot fabricate zero crosses out of ulps. Zero-line markers and zero-line alerts are both off by default.

Stepping toward zero is guaranteed to stay inside the band the pane displays; a “bullish up / bearish down” rule collapses onto the marker point whenever the cross sits on the same side of zero as its direction — the common case in trends — where the label ends up painted on the arrow inside a same-colour histogram cluster and reads as a missing label.

8.3 Bookkeeping rules

  • Keyed by source index, so a cross can never be marked twice and can never be missed when several chart bars share one source bar.
  • Deterministic anchor: the marker always sits on the chart bar that contains its source bar's open time — identical live, on a replay and after a reload. Anchoring the forming bar to the “current” chart bar instead made the position depend on when the cross first became visible intrabar: a replay and the live pass disagreed about the newest marker, and on charts finer than the source the marker slid rightwards while the bar formed.
  • Closed bars are back-filled exactly once, in order, and the back-fill is bounded by DrawRecentBars + 2 × MaxCrossMarkers bars — anything older would be trimmed immediately anyway, and on a deep history an unbounded walk meant a multi-second stall with every object alive at once. Trimming also runs during the walk, so peak memory stays flat.
  • The forming bar is provisional: re-evaluated on every tick, and its marker is withdrawn if the cross does not survive to the close.
  • Bounded by count (Max Cross Markers) and by the recent-history window.
  • Switching the markers off does not lose history: the back-fill watermark is parked at “nothing built”, so turning them back on rebuilds the visible window instead of waiting for a history reload.
  • After a reload everything is rebuilt from scratch, so markers can never be stranded or lost.

8.4 The trigger outputs

Bull Trigger and Bear Trigger are plotted at the MACD value of the triggering bar, so the dot sits exactly on the oscillator, and they are exposed as outputs so cBots can consume them (§12).

9. Multi-Timeframe Confluence Ladder

9.1 How the ladder is built

Two ways, and an explicit list always wins:

ModeBehaviour
Derived (Confluence Timeframes empty, default)the standard ladder M1, M2, M3, M5, M10, M15, M30, H1, H2, H3, H4, H6, H12, D1, D2, D3, W1, MN is walked from the resolved source timeframe upwards, and the first Confluence Levels (1–3) entries are used. Each level gets its OWN fully adaptive profile A confluence level is not the primary setting re-read on a coarser series: it is a complete pipeline built exactly the way the indicator would be if you had opened that chart yourself — its own resolved periods, its own norm window, its own volatility baseline. The ladder reports direction, so the regime filter bank is off at every level (a regime-mixed score would not be comparable across timeframes).

9.2 The state score

Each level (and the primary) is reduced to a single number in −100…+100 by three signed tests:

+/- 40 MACD line above / below zero +/- 35 MACD line above / below its signal line +/- 25 histogram rising / falling (against the previous bar) -------------------------------------------------- score = clamp(sum, -100, +100) (NaN -> the level is simply not available)

The ladder is then a weighted mean, with the primary timeframe carrying the most weight:

weights = { primary: 2.0, level 1: 1.0, level 2: 0.6, level 3: 0.4 } confluence = clamp( SUM(weight * score) / SUM(weight over available levels), -100, +100 )

A level that has fewer than three bars, no mapping, or no oscillator value yet is marked unavailable and contributes nothing — including nothing to the denominator, so a missing level cannot drag the score toward zero.

Textual states, used by the panel and the alert: BULL at ≥ +33, BEAR at ≤ −33, otherwise FLAT / NEUTRAL.

9.3 Mapping a source bar onto a higher timeframe

The same closed-bar rule as the primary mapping: “the last HTF bar that had closed by sourceOpenTime + sourceSpan”, answered as “opened at or before asOf − htfSpan”, with a walk-back guard for cutoffs that land inside a non-trading gap (the platform's lookup can round up there, and that bar had not closed).

Strict Non-Repaint MTF Mapping (default on) additionally excludes the newest HTF bar entirely. This matters on the live bar, where asOf is a moment in the future: without the exclusion the ladder quotes a higher-timeframe bar that is still forming, so the confluence score moves while the source bar is open — and after a history reload the same divergence would be re-scored against a now-closed HTF bar, i.e. a registered signal's quality would change retrospectively. With it, every quoted value is final and a replay reproduces it exactly. Turn it off if you want the ladder to follow the higher timeframe tick by tick and accept both consequences.

10.3 Geometry — the guarantee that it cannot cover the oscillator

  • Rows that fit are computed from the pane's real height minus margins, padding, border and the separator, divided by the row height implied by Panel Font Size. Zero is a legal answer: when even one row cannot fit, the card is hidden rather than allowed to spill outside the pane.
  • Columns that fit are computed from 78% of the pane width and the cell width, clamped to the built cell count.
  • In merged mode the row budget is shared between the two sections in proportion to what each actually wants to show, guaranteeing at least one row to whichever section has content, and hard-clamped so that no section can be asked to fill more rows than row controls exist (that would take the whole render pass down with an index error).
  • Priority selection: every item carries a priority (0 = must show, 1 = should, 2 = nice to have); the lowest priorities win, ties keep natural reading order, and the rest are dropped for this render. Shrinking the pane therefore removes the least important cells instead of overflowing.
  • The panel re-fits on the pane's SizeChanged event and refreshes no more often than Panel Refresh (ms), and only when something is actually dirty.
  • If native controls are unavailable or fail, everything degrades to two corner-anchored ChartStaticText objects with the same content and the same priority selection — the separator is then inserted between the two selected blocks, and carries the lowest possible priority so it can never win a slot ahead of real content.

11.3 Latching, arming and anti-spam

  • Nothing alerts during the initial history pass. Alerts are armed only when the first pass over the loaded history reaches the live edge, and the guard indices are seeded at that moment — so a crossover that happened while the chart was loading cannot fire an alert afterwards. A history reload re-arms the whole mechanism (§2.6).
  • One alert per event. Crossover and zero-line alerts are latched per evaluated source index; divergence alerts per end-pivot index; confluence flips per regime change (the sign latch), with at most one confluence alert per source bar.
  • A deferred flip is not a dropped flip. If the ladder flips twice inside one source bar, the second flip is announced on the next bar rather than silently discarded — and it is announced with the regime the sign latch actually tracks, so it can never announce a state that is no longer current.
  • Divergences must be live. A divergence alert only fires when the confirmation bar is within two bars of the live edge, so re-registering an old signal after a rebuild cannot produce a stale alert.
  • Divergences must clear the alert bar. Alert Min Quality is a separate, higher threshold than Min Quality Score: signals between the two are drawn and counted but stay silent. If your alert bar ends up below the quality gate, every passing signal alerts — and that is reported at start-up.
  • Closed-bar mode. Only On Closed Source Bar (default on) evaluates the last closed source bar, which makes alerts strictly non-repainting. Turn it off to be told inside the bar — the Scalping style does, because a scalper acts inside the bar — and accept that an intrabar alert can be withdrawn by the close.

14.3 Debug Log

Debug Log = true adds three things, and only three — it is not a firehose:

  • One line per rejected candidate, with the reason. The format is [Skip {Bull|Bear}{Hidden|Regular}] {reason} q={quality} @ {bar time UTC}; a hard-gate rejection reports q=0.0 because the candidate never reached the scoring stage. Real examples: [Skip BullHidden] ATR% 0.0091 below floor 0.0311 q=0.0 @ 2026-10-10 14:00, [Skip BearRegular] trend too strong to fade (-0.412) q=0.0 @ …, [Skip BullRegular] quality below threshold q=47.3 @ …, [Skip BullRegular] clustered with a previous signal q=0.0 @ …, [Skip BearHidden] outside the trading session (2026-10-10 22:15 UTC) q=0.0 @ …, [Skip …] duplicate end pivot q=0.0, [Skip …] follow-through 0.31 < 0.55 q=0.0, [Skip …] ADX gate (14.2) q=0.0, [Skip …] MTF gate (-41) q=0.0, [Skip …] hidden vs trend (-0.183) q=0.0. This is the answer to “why hasn't it fired in three days?”.
  • One line per registered divergence: [Div BullRegular A] q=82.4 priceLeg=1.31ATR oscLeg=0.27RMS dist=34 @ ….

20. Trading Divergences

20.1 Reading one signal in five seconds

  1. Colour: which family? Green/red = regular (reversal). Teal/orange = hidden (continuation). Amber = quality below your weak threshold, whatever the family. If you only trade reversals, you have just filtered half the labels without reading anything.
  2. Label: how good? The percentage is the 13-component score. Treat it as a ranking, not a probability: 90% means every component that could be evaluated came back strong.
  3. Grade (if shown): what kind of disagreement? A = the oscillator turned back through the zero line, or the family-aware delta is ≥ 0.60 of the oscillator's scale — genuine exhaustion. B = a clear but shallower disagreement. C = a diagonal, the weakest form.
  4. Position: where in the move? A regular bullish divergence wants price at a lower low after a downtrend, with the trend row reading DOWN or FLAT and momentum FADING. The same label printed halfway up a strong rally is a different (worse) trade.
  5. Context: what do the higher timeframes say? The MTF row. A bullish divergence against MTF −62 BEAR is a counter-trend scalp; with +62 BULL it is a pullback entry.

20.3 When to ignore a divergence that was drawn

  • It is amber. Below your weak threshold. The engine registered it because it cleared the floor; the colour says it is the weakest thing on the chart.
  • It is old. The panel's Age row is there for this. A divergence from 200 bars ago is history, not a signal — and the alert logic agrees: it only fires for signals whose confirmation bar is within two bars of the live edge.
  • The ATR row says WARM. The volatility gate has not armed yet, so the adaptive floor is not protecting you from dead-market pivots. On a freshly loaded chart, wait.
  • The family bucket's win rate is small-sample. Hid Bear 57% (7) is not information; the panel prints the sample size next to the percentage for exactly this reason.
  • Price is inside a spike. The volatility-regime component already marked it down (0.25 for a spike), but a spike also makes the pivots themselves less trustworthy than their prominence suggests.
The entry is the confirmation bar, not the pivot Everything the indicator measures — entry price, ATR, target, stop, the trend slope, the session — is taken at pivot + lookback, the first bar on which the pivot could have been known. When you compare your own results with the panel's win rate, use the same reference; entering at the pivot price is a number nobody can actually achieve, and it is the most common reason a manual tally disagrees with the measured one.

21. Trading Crossovers

21.1 What you are being told

A BUY/SELL marker is a transition of the crossover latch (§5.6). With Signal Sensitivity Lambda = 1.00 that is exactly the classic MACD/signal crossing. Below 1.00 it is an advanced trigger: the histogram has climbed out of a band measured in its own average size, on its way to a crossing that has not happened yet.

λBehaviourUse when
1.00 (default)the classic crossing; fewest false triggers, latest entryyou want comparability with any other MACD, or you trade swings
0.96–0.98a small advance; the trigger often lands one bar earlierintraday, when one bar of the move matters
0.92 (Scalping style)a real advance: the histogram must close 8% of its typical distance to zeroscalping, where waiting for the full cross costs more than the confirmation is worth
0.80 (minimum)aggressive; expect triggers inside consolidations that never complete the crossrarely. The latch guarantees no duplicates and no misses, but it cannot guarantee the crossing follows

21.2 The two things that make the advance safe

  • An early trigger may only fire in the direction the histogram is leaving, never the one it occupies — otherwise the latch would flip back on the next bar and print BUY/BUY pairs out of noise inside the band.
  • An early trigger is consumed by the crossing it anticipated, so the crossing itself does not print a second one. Exactly one trigger per oscillator excursion.

21.3 Live vs closed-bar marking

Mark Only Closed Bars = false (default) evaluates the forming source bar: you see the cross the moment it happens, and the marker is withdrawn if the cross does not survive to the close. Set it to true for a strictly non-repainting marker trail — the price of that is one bar of latency, which on H4 is four hours. Only On Closed Source Bar does the same job for alerts, independently.

21.4 Combining the two signal types

The highest-quality combination this product can show you A crossover in the direction of a recent high-quality divergence, with MTF agreeing and the ATR row reading OK. The divergence tells you the move is exhausting, the cross tells you it has turned, the ladder tells you the bigger picture is not against you, and the ATR row tells you the market is awake enough to pay for the trade. Three of the four alone is a discretionary trade; all four is what the gates were built to find.

22. Multi-Timeframe Workflow

  1. Decide what the ladder is for. Directional context (keep SoftPenalty) or a hard veto (HardGate). Soft is the better default: a divergence against the higher timeframe is not worthless, it just has to earn a higher quality score.
  2. Let it derive, or pin it. Empty Confluence Timeframes takes the next Confluence Levels timeframes above the source from the standard ladder. Pin it when you have a view — an H1 trader who cares about H4 and D1 and nothing else should say H4,D1.
  3. Check the ladder actually resolved. The log prints MTF ladder: H4 D1, and the panel shows one row per available level. A missing row means that series has too few bars or no closed mapping yet — not an error, and it contributes nothing rather than dragging the score toward zero.
  4. Read the score, not the state. BULL/BEAR/NEUTRAL are the ±33 bands; the number tells you how much of the ladder agrees. +100 means every level is above zero, above its signal and rising.
  5. Keep strict mapping on unless you specifically want the intrabar view. With it on, the ladder only ever quotes higher-timeframe bars that have really closed, so a registered signal's confluence — and therefore its quality — cannot change after a history reload.
  6. Remember each level is independently adaptive. An H4 level under an M5 primary is not your M5 settings read on H4 data; it is the profile the indicator would resolve if you opened H4 directly. That is why the ladder stays meaningful across a wide timeframe span.

23. The Gates in Practice — Loosening and Tightening

23.1 The order to investigate in

  1. Turn on Debug Log. Every rejection prints its reason and, where it is numeric, the value against the threshold: [Skip BullHidden] ATR% 0.0091 below floor 0.0311 q=0.0 @ …, [Skip BearRegular] trend too strong to fade (-0.412) q=0.0 @ …, [Skip BullRegular] quality below threshold q=47.3 @ ….
  2. Read the reasons for the last dozen rejections. If they are all the same gate, that gate is your binding constraint — change it and nothing else.
  3. Change one thing, then re-read. Every gate interacts with the quality score, so changing three at once tells you nothing.

24. Alerts Workflow

  1. Choose the channels. Write Alerts To Log, Show Alert Popup, Play Sound — three independent switches under the Enable Alerts master. The popup is the one that reaches you when the terminal is in the background; it is colour-coded green for bullish and red for bearish.
  2. Choose the triggers. Crossovers (on by default) are the frequent ones; divergences (on) are the selective ones; zero-line crosses and confluence flips are off by default and both are noisy on fast charts.
  3. Set the alert bar. Alert Min Quality (default 70) is deliberately above Min Quality Score (default 50): you want to see more signals on the chart than you want to be interrupted about. The start-up log tells you which side of the quality gate your alert bar ended up on.
  4. Decide about intrabar alerts. Only On Closed Source Bar (default on) makes alerts strictly non-repainting. Off is earlier and can be contradicted by the close.
  5. Know the arming rule. Nothing alerts during the initial history pass — otherwise attaching the indicator would replay every historical crossover at you. Alerts arm when the first pass reaches the live edge, and the guard indices are seeded at that moment.
Alert rates At most one crossover alert and one zero-line alert per evaluated source bar, one divergence alert per end pivot, and one confluence alert per regime change (with at most one per source bar — a second flip inside the same bar is deferred to the next bar, not dropped). A history reload re-arms everything and cannot fire stale alerts.

25. Reading Your Own Statistics

  • Always read the sample size with the percentage. 58% (43) is a fact about your feed; 75% (4) is a coincidence. Min Learning Samples exists to keep the second kind out of the quality score, but the panel shows both so you can judge for yourself.
  • Compare the four buckets, not the average. The families are different hypotheses. A configuration that is profitable on Reg Bull and hopeless on Hid Bear should be traded with Divergence Kinds narrowed — that is what the parameter is for.
  • Expired is not a loss. A signal that reached neither target nor stop inside the horizon is unresolved. The win rate is wins / (wins + losses); expired signals are excluded, because folding them into either side would invent a result.
  • The default target/stop is deliberately conservative. 1.0 ATR against 1.5 ATR means the measured win rate is the one a tight-target/loose-stop trader experiences. If you trade the other way round, set Outcome Target and Outcome Stop to your own ratio before you start judging the numbers.
  • Statistics reset on a history reload. They are a statistic of the records in the current window; keeping them across a reload would count the same market events twice and inflate the sample counts.
  • They persist per symbol and per timeframe. AMP|EURUSD|h1 and AMP|EURUSD|m15 are different stores, so switching timeframe does not mix two populations.
  • Avg qual is your selectivity gauge. If it sits just above Min Quality Score, the floor is your binding constraint and most candidates are being rejected at the last step.

27. The Golden Rules

  1. Read the start-up log before you read the chart. It prints the configuration actually in force — resolved periods, both kernels, gates, windows, warm-up estimate — and every contradiction it reconciled. Two minutes there saves a week of guessing why the indicator “ignores” a setting.
  2. Treat “(reference)” parameters as anchors. In automatic mode you are moving a curve, not a number. If you want literal periods, switch Adaptation Mode to Manual — and then read the drift warnings, because manual mode promises to honour what you typed.
  3. Never change two gates at once. The quality score is a weighted mean; changing two inputs tells you nothing about either. Change one, look at Avg qual and the per-family win rates, then decide.
  4. Sample size beats percentage. Every win rate in this product is printed with its n. Respect it.
  5. Family matters. A regular bullish and a hidden bearish divergence are different hypotheses with different track records. Narrow Divergence Kinds to what your bucket statistics actually support.
  6. Quality is a ranking, not a probability. 92% means “every component that could be evaluated came back strong”. It does not mean 92 chances in 100.
  7. Use the confirmation bar as your reference. That is where the indicator measures entry, ATR, target and stop. Comparing your fills to the pivot price compares yourself to a trade nobody can take.
  8. Give the pane room. The panel fits itself to the pane height and hides low-priority cells first; a two-row-tall pane will show you almost nothing and that is by design, not a bug.
  9. Keep strict MTF mapping on unless you have a specific reason. It is what makes a registered signal's quality stable across history reloads.
  10. Let the warm-up finish before judging. The oscillator, the ATR gate and the learning buckets all need bars. A chart attached ten minutes ago on M1 knows nothing yet, and the panel says so (WARM, collecting).
  11. Turn learning off when comparing configurations. Adaptive Learning = false makes the detector a pure function of parameters and data, which is the only fair way to A/B two setups.
  12. If you see nothing, read the skip reasons. Debug Log = true prints one line per rejected candidate with the number that rejected it. “No signals” is always a specific gate, and it is named.

30. Performance & Chart Hygiene

  • The core is incremental. A tick re-evaluates one source bar. Simple-MA windows are O(1) per tick (the cache remembers the value it holds at the forming index and swaps it, re-deriving exactly every 1024 steps); recursive kernels are O(1) by construction; composite kernels pay for their cascade.
  • The heaviest choices, in order: Price Input = VolumePriceAdjusted (an O(window) scan per moving tick, memoised when nothing moved), long Slow Period with Triangular or Hull kernels, a large NormWindow (derived: 4×Slow), and three confluence levels (each a complete pipeline).
  • Drawing budgets are the thing to tune on deep histories. Draw Only Most Recent N Bars and Max Drawn Divergences bound divergence objects; Max Cross Markers and the same recent-bars window bound markers. The marker back-fill after a reload is bounded by DrawRecentBars + 2×MaxCrossMarkers, and trimming runs during the walk, so a deep history cannot stall the first paint or hold thousands of objects alive.
  • Object names are stable and namespaced per instance. No flicker, no leaks, and two copies of the indicator on one chart never fight over the same object.
  • Records outlive their drawings on purpose. A divergence whose chart objects were evicted keeps its record, because outcomes, learning, the panel census and the alerts all key off source indices. The list is capped at max(MaxDivergences, LearningMemory) + 64, and a record with no objects holds plain values only.
  • Panel refresh is throttled (Panel Refresh (ms), default 250) and skipped entirely when nothing is dirty. Raising it to 500–1000 on a busy multi-chart workspace is free readability.
  • Turn off what you do not read. Every section switch (Show Configuration/Market/MTF/Signals/Statistics Section) removes work as well as ink; Show Divergences = false stops the pivot scan entirely.

31. Verifying the Non-Repaint Claim Yourself

The claim is specific, so the test can be too. Do this once on a symbol you care about; it takes five minutes.

  1. Record the state. On a chart with a decent history, note the three most recent divergence labels (family, quality, position) and the last few BUY/SELL markers. A screenshot is enough.
  2. Force a rebuild. Scroll the chart far to the left so the platform downloads deeper history, then back to the live edge. The indicator detects the change and rebuilds everything from scratch (§2.6).
  3. Compare. The same divergences must be there, with the same quality numbers, the same colours and the same anchors. If a quality number changed, Strict Non-Repaint MTF Mapping is off and the confluence component was re-scored against a now-closed higher-timeframe bar — which is exactly what the switch is for.
  4. Switch timeframe and back. Same result; the rebuild path is the same one.
  5. Watch the forming bar. A crossover marker on the live bar may appear and then disappear if the cross does not survive to the close. That is the documented provisional behaviour, not repainting of a confirmed signal: closed bars are back-filled exactly once and never revisited.
  6. Check a pivot's publication time. A pivot at bar c can only be acted upon from bar c + Pivot Lookback onwards, and the divergence dot sits at that confirmation bar — never at the pivot. Count the bars between the pivot and the dot: it is the lookback, every time.
  7. Replay it if your build has bar-by-bar replay. Step through the last 200 bars. Signals must appear at the same bars with the same labels as the static chart shows — the pivot scanner advances confluence, volatility and outcomes to their per-confirmation state precisely so that a bulk pass and a live pass agree.

Does it repaint?Confirmed signals do not: pivots publish only after their right leg closes, outcomes latch, marker anchors come from bar open times, and a history reload rebuilds rather than patches. Why is the panel missing?Either Show Info Panel is off, or the pane is too short for even one row — in which case the card hides itself rather than covering the plot. Drag the pane taller
Why does ATR say WARM?The adaptive volatility model needs max(VolWindow/2, 30) samples before it will state a percentile floor. Until then the gate is inert (permissive) rather than guessing — a floor estimated from a handful of bars silently deletes valid signals

The score is designed to mean “strong for this instrument and timeframe”, not “the same number everywhere”
Why are hidden divergences a different colour?So a continuation claim is never mistaken for a reversal at a glance. Teal = hidden bullish, orange = hidden bearish, green/red = regular, amber = below the weak threshold in any family
Where are my popups?Show Alert Popup must be on and the build must support the custom-notification API. If it does not, one NOTE is printed at the first attempt and the channel disables itself — the log line and the sound still fire. Sounds never play during backtesting or optimisation
I turned the markers off and back on. Are the old ones lost?No. The back-fill watermark is parked at “nothing built” while the layer is off, so re-enabling rebuilds the visible window. (An earlier build advanced the watermark instead, which lost every older marker until a history reload)
Can a cBot read the signals?Yes — eleven chart-indexed output series (§12). The four event series (bull/bear trigger, bull/bear divergence) are the machine-readable channel; NaN means “no event on this bar”. The divergence dot is placed at the confirmation bar, so acting on it is not look-ahead
Does it work on tick / renko / range charts?Yes, with an estimated bar duration (median of up to 2000 open-time deltas), because those charts have no fixed one. Everything derived inherits the approximation. For exactness, keep the chart and set Source Timeframe to a time-based timeframe
Two copies on one chart — do they collide?No. Object names are namespaced with the instance id, and the learning store is keyed by symbol and resolved profile. Move one panel to another corner so they do not overlap visually
Why is the first attach on a deep history slower than the next ones?One full pass over the loaded bars, plus marker back-fill within its budget and the initial outcome scan. After that everything is incremental. The budgets of §30 are what keep that pass bounded
What does (reference / manual) in a parameter name mean?That the value is a reference anchored at H1 and rescaled in automatic mode, but taken literally in manual mode. It is in the name so the dialog cannot mislead you
