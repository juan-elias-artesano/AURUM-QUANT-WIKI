SECTION 1 — FEATURES

1. Introduction & Philosophy

1.1 The problem with a naked RSI

Wilder’s Relative Strength Index is one of the most studied oscillators in existence, and one of the most misused. Out of the box it has three failure modes that every trader eventually meets:

  • It is timeframe-blind. A 14-period RSI on M1 averages 14 minutes of flow; on D1 it averages three trading weeks. The same number on the dial means two completely different markets, yet every textbook rule (“fade 70/30”) is written as if the dial meant one thing.
  • Its zones are static. 70/30 was calibrated on 1970s daily commodities. In a volatile regime the oscillator parks above 70 for days (a trending market, not an “overbought” one); in a quiet regime it never reaches the zones at all, so the rules never fire.
  • Its signals are unmeasured. A divergence either “looks good” or it does not. Nothing tells you, after a hundred of them on your instrument and your broker’s feed, whether they have been worth acting on — so confidence is a matter of mood.

Advanced RSI Pro attacks all three. The first with a scaling law that resolves every window from the actual bar duration (§3); the second with volatility-adaptive zones that breathe with the market’s own ATR% (§5.6); the third with a quality score for every divergence (§6.6) and an adaptive learner that keeps score of every signal after the fact (§7).

1.2 Design philosophy

  1. The classic reading stays available. With Adaptation Mode = Manual, Dynamic Levels off, Regime-Adaptive Filter Bank off and Signal Sensitivity Lambda = 1.00, the main line is textbook Wilder RSI, computed on the raw close, with a simple-moving-average signal line. Nothing is hidden behind the adaptive machinery — you can always get back to the oscillator you already know.
  2. One truth per number. Every zone-dependent feature — cross markers, divergence zone checks, MTF state scoring, the panel — reads the same live band buffers that are drawn on the pane. There is no second, private 70/30 anywhere in the engine.
  3. Non-repainting by construction. Pivots are confirmed only after Pivot Lookback further bars have closed; divergences, plans, trendline breaks and (by default) cross markers are all resolved from closed source bars. A glyph that appears on your chart never moves, never disappears and never changes its meaning afterwards (§2.2, §35).
  4. Missing data never blocks and never invents. Every gate family treats an unavailable reading (warm-up, a missing volume feed, a corrupt quote) as unknown: the term is excluded from scores instead of being replaced by a constant, and a non-finite quote is skipped rather than allowed to fabricate a signal (Appendix B).
  5. Measure yourself. The learning engine and the CSV journal exist so that the tool’s own claims can be audited on your feed, in your session, with your settings — not taken on faith (§7, §16, §29).

1.3 What the indicator is — and is not

It is A decision-support instrument: an adaptive oscillator, a divergence detector with graded quality, a multi-timeframe confluence read, explicit trade plans with managed lifecycles, and a statistics panel that reports how its own signals have performed.
It is not An auto-trader. It places no orders, modifies none, reads no account or position data, and requests AccessRights.None. A “BUY” marker is an annotation of a measured condition, not an instruction. Trading involves substantial risk of loss; nothing in this manual is investment advice.

2.3 Timeframe mapping: the four families

Whenever the engine translates between the chart’s bars and the source (or a rung’s) bars it uses one of four mapping families, all built on the same hybrid-closure rule: a bar counts as closed at time asOf iff min(nominal close, successor open) ≤ asOf. On regular calendars this is exactly the nominal close; on irregular ones (Monthly bars, holiday-shortened days, weekend gaps) the successor’s open proves the bar closed earlier than its nominal span claims, which is what kills look-ahead.

  • MapChartToSource — chart bar → the source bar that contains it (used for marker placement and trigger outputs).
  • MapByCloseTime — used when the source is finer than the chart, so several source bars close inside one chart bar.
  • SourceToChartIndex — source bar → the chart bar to draw on.
  • MapToClosedIndex — the highest closed index of a rung as of a source bar’s close; honours Strict Non-Repaint MTF Mapping (never returns the still-forming last bar).
Strict vs non-strict With Strict Non-Repaint MTF Mapping = Yes (default) a rung’s in-progress bar is never read: the ladder sees each higher timeframe exactly as it stood when your source bar closed. Switching it off lets the last (still forming) rung bar contribute, which is one tick fresher and one tick less honest — the panel will show it, but the recorded, journaled and alerted values stay closed-bar based.

3. Timeframe Resolution & the Adaptive Engine

3.1 What gets resolved, and when

At start-up (and whenever history length changes materially) the engine measures the source timeframe’s real bar span — exactly, from the timeframe name for named periods; as the sampled median of recent bar deltas for name-less ones (Tick / Renko / Range) — and builds an AdaptiveProfile: the complete set of effective windows and thresholds. Every confluence rung gets its own profile, resolved from its own bar span, which is why a Weekly rung behaves like a Weekly indicator even though you never configured one.

3.2 The scaling law

With d = bar minutes, ε = Microstructure Noise Horizon (min) and e = the scaling exponent of Timeframe Scaling:

noiseRatio = (1 + ε/d) / (1 + ε/60) horizonRatio = 60 / d scale = noiseRatio^0.5 · horizonRatio^e (clamped to [floor .. Max Period Multiplier]) floor = 1.00 (High Timeframe Policy = Canonical) floor = 0.35 (High Timeframe Policy = HorizonMatched) exponent e = 0.00 BarCount (windows stay in bars) = 0.15 Balanced (default: gentle compromise) = 1.00 TimeHorizon (windows track real time)

Each window then scales as round(reference · scale^k · gain) with its own exponent k and clamp range, and gain = 1 / Global Sensitivity (a higher sensitivity shortens the RSI-family windows). The exponents are deliberately unequal:

After scaling, calendar ceilings bound the coarse end: Trend EMA ≤ 730 days of bars, Max Pivot Distance ≤ 730 d, Min Pivot Distance and Trendline Min Gap ≤ 180 d, Outcome Horizon ≤ 120 d, Stats Window ≤ 365 d, Draw Window ≤ 1095 d. Final normalisations keep the profile self-consistent: band width ≥ 15 points, MinPivotDistance ≥ 2 × PivotLookback, MaxPivotDistance > MinPivotDistance, WeakQuality ≥ MinQuality, AlertQuality ≥ MinQuality, ErSmoothing = 2 × ErPeriod (clamped 5–100), and a 10-day volatility window in [150–3000] bars.

3.3 Zone bias and quality bias

Two small logarithmic biases keep the zones and the gates honest across timeframes:

zoneBias = clamp( 0.5 · log2(60 / d), -0.05, +0.05 ) OB += zoneBias · 10 OS -= zoneBias · 10 (fine bars: slightly wider zones) qualBias = clamp( 0.2 · log2(60 / d), -0.10, +0.10 ) MinQuality, WeakQuality, AlertQuality ×= (1 + qualBias)

On H1 both biases are exactly zero — the reference timeframe is untouched. On M1 the zones widen by at most ±0.5 points and the quality floor rises by at most 10 %, which is the engine’s way of saying “fine-timeframe noise needs a slightly higher bar to be called a signal”.

3.4 Manual mode

Adaptation Mode = Manual bypasses the scaling law entirely: every reference parameter is used verbatim (clamped to its declared min/max), on every timeframe. Use it when you want the literal textbook configuration (RSI 14 / signal 9 / 70-30) on one specific timeframe and accept that the same numbers mean something different if you switch the chart period. The panel’s TF cell shows “manual” in that case.

3.5 The noise horizon

Microstructure Noise Horizon (min) (ε) models the idea that each bar carries a fixed slice of pure microstructure noise (spreads, auction gaps, quote granularity). With ε = 0 the scale reduces to the pure horizon ratio; raising ε increases the scale on fine timeframes (longer windows there) because a larger share of each M1 bar is noise. The default 1.0 minute is a conservative FX/crypto value; leave it unless you know your instrument’s noise floor.

5. The RSI Core

5.1 Pipeline overview

price[i] = f(O,H,L,C,Volume) ← RSI Price Input (§5.2) gain / loss = max(±(price[i]-price[i-1]), 0) rsiFast = Wilder(gain,loss; P) ← P = resolved RSI Period rsiSlow = Wilder(gain,loss; P·(1+spread)) ← only with Regime-Adaptive Filter Bank blend = w·rsiFast + (1-w)·rsiSlow, w = EMA(Kaufman ER; ErSmoothing) rsi = SuperSmoother(blend) clamped [0..100] ← if Use SuperSmoother signal = SMA(rsi; Signal Period) stochRSI = (rsi - min(rsi,n)) / (max(rsi,n) - min(rsi,n)) · 100 ATR% = WilderATR(TR; ATR Period)/close · 100 ; baseline = SMA(ATR%; Level Baseline) OB/OS bands = static profile levels, stretched by ATR%/baseline (§5.6)

5.3 Wilder legs and the regime-adaptive filter bank

The gain/loss averages use Wilder’s recursion (seed = the first full-window SMA, then avg += (x - avg)/P). With Regime-Adaptive Filter Bank = No (default, and default in every preset) there is a single leg at the resolved RSI Period — the classic computation.

With the bank on, a second, slower leg (period × (1+spread)) and a faster leg (period × (1−spread)) are blended by the smoothed Kaufman Efficiency Ratio of the raw close: trending bars (ER→1) trust the fast leg, choppy bars (ER→0) the slow one. Filter Bank Spread sets the leg separation (0.35 → fast = 0.65P, slow = 1.35P). An unavailable ER reads as w = 0.5 (neutral), never as a fabricated regime.

The bank is off by default for a reason It changes the meaning of the main line (it is no longer Wilder’s RSI) and therefore of every zone, divergence and statistic built on it. Turn it on deliberately, on one chart, and compare — do not enable it because a parameter exists.

5.4 SuperSmoother

Use SuperSmoother (default on) passes the blended RSI through Ehlers’ two-pole super smoother at SuperSmoother Period, clamped to [0..100]. It removes most of the one-bar grit of fine timeframes while keeping far less lag than an SMA of the same period. With it off, the main line is exactly the (blended) Wilder RSI. During warm-up, before the smoother has two bars of history, the unsmoothed value is passed through, so the line never starts empty.

5.5 Signal line and StochRSI

  • Signal = simple moving average of the final RSI over the resolved Signal Period. It is the “trigger line” of the MACD-style cross family (§8) and a momentum vote in the MTF state score (§9.3). Display toggle: Show Signal Line.
  • StochRSI = the position of the current RSI inside its own high/low range over the resolved Stoch Period, in 0–100. A flat range (width < 1e-9) resolves to 50 rather than dividing by zero. It serves as a divergence confirmation vote (§6.5) and a fast early-warning line. Display toggle: Show Stoch RSI Line.

5.6 Dynamic OB/OS levels (ATR)

With Dynamic Levels (ATR) on (default), the overbought / oversold bands are not constants: they breathe with realised volatility. The engine compares the bar’s ATR% of price with its own long-run baseline (Volatility Baseline (bars)):

ratio = ATR%[i] / baseline[i] stretch = clamp( Level Adjustment Factor · (ratio - 1), -4, +4 ) shift = clamp( stretch · (OB - OS) · 0.10, ±((OB-OS)/2 - 10) ) OB[i] = clamp( OB + shift, min(55, OB), 95 ) OS[i] = clamp( OS - shift, 5, max(45, OS) ) width = OB[i] - OS[i] ≥ 15 (else re-centred at ±7.5)

Hot regimes push the bands outward (a trending, volatile market must reach further to be called “over”); quiet regimes pull them inward so the zones remain reachable. At zero stretch the dynamic bands are bit-identical to the static ones for every configuration, and the dotted Overbought / Oversold output series show exactly the bands every consumer reads. The panel reports them live (e.g. 68.8/31.2 dyn). Level Adjustment Factor scales the breathing (0 = static behaviour with dynamic plumbing; the presets use 1.5–3.0).

5.7 Zone, midline and signal-line state machines

Three binary state buffers underlie every cross in the engine, all computed per closed bar:

  • In-OB / In-OS with lambda hysteresis: the zone is entered at the band itself and left only band = (1 - lambda) · (OB - OS) · 0.10 points through the band. Signal Sensitivity Lambda = 1.00 is the classic exact-level cross; 0.92 (Scalping) demands a 0.32-point confirmed exit on 70/30 bands — enough to discard most border whipsaws at the cost of a slightly later trigger.
  • Signal-line side: sign(RSI − signal), sticky at exact equality.
  • Midline side: sign(RSI − 50), sticky at exact equality.

5.8 Output scale

Output Scale = Raw plots the oscillator in classic 0–100. Centered subtracts 50, so the pane reads −50..+50 with the midline at zero — convenient when you stack it under other centred oscillators. Every drawn object, tooltip and trigger output follows the chosen scale (the panel and alert messages deliberately keep quoting raw 0–100 readings as event records). The higher-TF RSI line is plotted in the primary line’s display units in both modes.

5.9 The higher-TF RSI line and the trend-bias line

  • RSI Higher TF (dotted orange): the first confluence rung’s RSI, strict-mapped to closed rung bars and plotted in the primary display units — a permanent window into “what would the RSI say one timeframe up?” Toggle: Show Higher-TF RSI Line.

6.2 Pivots: the atoms of every pattern

A pivot is a strict fractal extremum of the RSI line itself (not of price): bar c is a high pivot if its RSI is strictly greater than the RSI of the Pivot Lookback bars on each side (ties tolerated on one side only — at least one strictly lower neighbour per side is required). A pivot confirms at bar c + Pivot Lookback: that is the first bar at which the fractal condition can be known, and the first bar at which the engine is allowed to act on it. Nothing is ever drawn or registered earlier.

Each pivot carries a prominence: how far its RSI stands out from the stronger of its two neighbours’ extremes, converted to ATR units of price ((prominence/100) / (ATR/close)). Pivots below Pivot Prominence (x ATR) are noise and never enter a pool. The pools keep the most recent MaxPivotDistance + 2·lookback + 16 (minimum 400) pivots per side.

6.3 Pairing, legs and zones

When a new pivot confirms, the detector walks the same-side pool from newest to oldest, keeping pairs whose distance lies in [Min Pivot Distance .. Max Pivot Distance] bars, evaluating at most Max Pivot Candidates pairs and keeping the best-ranked one. A pair survives only if:

  • the price leg is at least Min Price Leg (x ATR) ATR long — a divergence on a flat price leg is a rounding artefact;
  • the RSI leg is at least Min RSI Leg (points) points long;
  • with Require Extreme Zone on: for regular classes at least one of the two pivots reached beyond the live band (with a ±5-point tolerance); for hidden classes the pair must not sit entirely in the opposite extreme zone (a “hidden bull” with both pivots above the overbought band is a contradiction).

6.7 Registration, drawing and deduplication

A registered record stores everything: both pivots’ indices, times, prices and RSI readings, the confirmation bar, quality, confluence snapshot, legs, entry price and ATR, and (from v3.0) the trade plan and its lifecycle. Records are drawn as a line between the two pivots plus optional markers, drop lines and labels (Draw Style, Show Drop Lines, Show Labels, wording and grade/quality toggles in group 8), optionally mirrored onto the price chart (Mirror Divergences On Price Chart).

Two caps keep the pane readable: at most Max Drawn Divergences drawn records (newest win), and only records whose end pivot lies within Draw Only Most Recent N Bars source bars (the window is re-enforced continuously, not just at registration). A four-slot dedup watermark (side × class) prevents the same end pivot from registering twice.

7. Outcomes & Adaptive Learning

7.1 The measurement contract

Every registered divergence is a forecast, and the engine grades every forecast afterwards on the ATR ruler: entry = the close of the confirmation bar; then, bar by bar over Outcome Horizon (reference) source bars, the excursion is tracked in units of the entry bar’s ATR:

  • WIN — the favourable excursion reaches Outcome Target (x ATR) first;
  • LOSS — the adverse excursion reaches Outcome Stop (x ATR) first; if one bar touches both, the stop is assumed to fill first (the conservative same-bar rule, identical to the plan engine’s);
  • EXPIRED — neither level was touched within the horizon.

Along the way the engine records MFE (best favourable excursion) and MAE (worst adverse excursion) in ATR units — the raw material of the panel’s Path and MFE/MAE cells.

Two rulers, two names — zero confusion The ATR ruler (Outcome Target / Stop) grades outcomes and feeds the statistics. The trade-plan ruler (§13) draws levels as R-multiples of the plan’s own risk. They are independent by design since v3.2; the panel labels the ATR ruler explicitly as Out (ATR).

7.2 The four learning buckets

Resolved outcomes accumulate in four buckets — regular-bull, hidden-bull, regular-bear, hidden-bear — so a family that works on your feed lifts its own future quality scores (the 0.02 weight) and shows its own win rate in the panel. Each bucket keeps Learning Memory (outcomes) resolved samples: when the cap is exceeded the win / loss / expired counters are scaled down proportionally (exact double arithmetic — the v3.7 fix that removed the rounding ratchet which used to drag saturated buckets to 50/50).

With Persist Learning (LocalStorage) on, the buckets survive restarts (saved at most every 5 seconds and on shutdown, scope = indicator type). Corrupt or hand-edited storage is rejected field by field: non-finite literals, negatives and oversized counters are discarded without poisoning the session. A restored state counts as “seeded” only if it contains at least one win or loss — an all-expired file must not suppress the new session’s own learning.

Min Learning Samples is the number of resolved outcomes a bucket needs before its win rate is allowed to influence quality at all; below it the term is simply absent. Switching Adaptive Learning off freezes and hides the whole layer (the panel shows collecting / off).

9. Multi-Timeframe Confluence Ladder

9.1 Building the ladder

With Enable MTF Confluence on, the engine stacks the primary source timeframe plus Confluence Levels (1–3) higher rungs. Auto mode walks the standard ladder (M1, M2, M3, M5, M10, M15, M30, H1, H2, H3, H4, H6, H12, D1, D2, D3, W1, MN) and picks the next timeframe at least 3.2× the current bar span — a separation wide enough that rungs are genuinely independent votes. A rung that cannot be materialised (unsupported period, no server data) consumes no slot: the ladder silently steps further up and the panel shows only the rungs that exist. Confluence Timeframes accepts an explicit list (e.g. H4, D1; comma / semicolon / space / pipe separated; M/H/D/W/MN shorthands, full words and Heikin names accepted) and overrides the auto walk.

Each rung is a full pipeline of its own: its own resolved adaptive profile (its own bar span!), its own price input (yours), its own dynamic bands — exactly as if you had opened that chart and dropped the indicator on it. Rungs load enough history for their own windows (weekly trend EMAs included), which is why Auto Load History / Min Source Bars matter.

9.2 The weighted score

score = ( 2.0·state(primary) + 1.0·state(rung1) + 0.6·state(rung2) + 0.4·state(rung3) ) / (sum of weights of the rungs that produced a reading) ∈ [-100 .. +100]

The primary source always carries weight 2.0 — confluence informs, it does not overrule your chart. The panel shows the score and a three-state verdict: ≥ +33 BULL, ≤ -33 BEAR, else NEUTRAL, plus one cell per rung (e.g. H4 +51 BULL).

9.3 How one timeframe votes: the state score

Each rung’s state is a ±100 score built from four additive votes, computed against the rung’s own live bands:

position : (rsi-50)·2 inside the bands; above OB: (OB-50)·2 - (rsi-OB)·0.5 (rising into OB still bullish, but saturating) below OS: (OS-50)·2 + (OS-rsi)·0.5 (falling into OS still bearish, but saturating) momentum : +25 if rsi rose last bar, -25 if it fell signal : +20 if rsi > its signal line, -20 if below exhaustion : -15 if rsi ≥ OB+10 (overextended up), +15 if rsi ≤ OS-10 (overextended down) → clamped to [-100 .. +100]

The curve is continuous at every band border for any band placement, and at the classic 70/30 it reproduces the original anchors exactly (RSI 50 → 0, 70 rising → +65, 80 exhausted → +45…). The exhaustion vote is what makes the ladder useful in trends: a Weekly RSI parked at 78 votes less bullish than one at 65, because that is what “overbought” should mean in a confluence vote.

9.4 The three gate modes

Confluence GateEffect on divergences

SoftPenalty (default)The aligned ladder score (±score by signal direction) enters the quality score as a small 3 % term around the Min Confluence Score threshold — opposing flow dents quality, supporting flow lifts it; nothing is hard-rejected.
HardGateAdditionally rejects any divergence whose aligned score is below 2·MinConfluenceScore - 100 (e.g. Min 45 → aligned < −10 rejected). The Position style uses this.

Independently, Hidden: MTF Alignment Gate always rejects hidden (continuation) divergences when the aligned ladder score is ≤ −33 — the classic “hidden bear printed into a higher-timeframe melt-up” trap.

9.5 The confluence-flip alert

The ladder’s three-state verdict (BULL / NEUTRAL / BEAR) is a first-class event: when it flips between bullish and bearish states the engine can alert (§11), evaluated on closed source bars only — a flip that exists only inside an in-progress bar never rings.

11.3 Timing rules (why alerts never double-fire or vanish)

  • Closed-bar gate. Only On Closed Source Bar (default on) evaluates the cross families at s-1; divergence, plan-level and trendline alerts are closed-bar by construction. The Scalping preset relaxes this for crosses and alerts together with its live markers.
  • Watermark scans. Every elapsed closed source bar since the last evaluation is scanned (most recent win), so a source finer than the chart cannot hide a cross inside an hour.
  • Ratio-scaled freshness. An event older than the freshness window — max(8, 2 × source bars per chart bar) for cross alerts and max(2, ratio) for record-keyed alerts (divergence, plan level, trendline break) — is treated as stale history and not announced (its marker and journal line still exist). After a weekend or a disconnect you get the state, not a flood of ancient rings.
  • Cooldown. Alert Cooldown (source bars, 0 = off) spaces repeats per direction: a cooldown of 3 silences a second bullish cross within 3 source bars while a bearish one can still ring.
  • History gate. Nothing alerts during the start-up / reload history rescan — only live events ring. Since v3.24.6 the rescan counts as finished only when the feed has genuinely caught up to the clock (the last source bar opened within ~10 bar spans — a 15-minute floor for sub-minute sources — of real time, tightened from a 5-day floor in v3.24.7), so even a platform that streams the backlog in after the attach cannot leak historical events through as “live” ones.

13. Trade Management — the Projection Engine

13.1 What a plan is

Every registered divergence (not only the drawn ones) receives a full trade plan at its confirmation bar: an ENTRY (the confirmation close), a STOP, and two targets TP1 / TP2 expressed as R-multiples of the plan’s own risk. The plan is drawn on the price chart as four labelled horizontal levels spanning Projection Length (chart bars), echoed in the panel, included in alerts and written to the journal. It is a projection — a “if you acted on this signal, here is the coherent ruler” — never an order.

13.2 Stop placement: two sources, one sanity band

  • PivotStructure (default): the stop sits beyond the market structure that produced the signal — the divergence’s extreme pivot (the lower of the two pivots’ prices for a bull, the higher for a bear) plus Pivot Stop Buffer (x ATR). If the structure lies on the wrong side of the entry or closer than 0.25 ATR, the ATR ruler takes over.
  • AtrMultiple: the stop is Outcome Stop (x ATR) × entry ATR — exactly the ruler the learning engine grades outcomes with, so what you see is what the win rate describes.

Whichever source wins, the final risk is clamped to [0.5 .. 6] ATR of the entry — stops can never be noise-tight on a violent instrument nor pattern-loose on a quiet one. Targets then follow: TP1 = entry + Plan TP1 (R multiple) × risk, TP2 = entry + Plan TP2 (R multiple) × risk (defaults 1R / 2R; the v3.2 geometry release made reward≥risk the default on purpose).

13.3 Drawing and the visible-plan budget

At most Max Visible Trade Plans plans are drawn at once (newest first; the registry itself keeps every plan for its lifecycle and for the statistics). Labels (Show Projection Labels) read ENTRY, SL -1R, TP1 +1.0R, TP2 +2.0R; colours come from Entry Line Color / Stop Line Color and the signal direction’s colour for the targets. A plan whose confirmation bar scrolls out of the chart window is removed and retried every extension pass, so zooming back never loses it permanently.

13.4 The lifecycle state machine

RUNNING → TP1 HIT (still running) → TP2 HIT (terminal) ↘ STOPPED (terminal) ↘ STALE (terminal: 4 × Outcome Horizon bars elapsed)
  • Evaluated on closed source bars only, strictly chronologically, from the bar after confirmation.
  • Same-bar conservatism: if one bar can touch both the stop and a target, the stop is assumed to fill first (a LOSS / STOPPED) — identical to the outcome engine’s rule, so the two never disagree.
  • A bar touching TP2 while still RUNNING credits TP1 on the same bar (conservative: the half-position that would have paid at TP1 is counted), then terminates as TP2 HIT.
  • Reached levels turn dotted and carry suffixes: TP1 +1.0R HIT, TP2 +2.0R VOID, SL -1R HIT, ENTRY ... STALE — a finished plan never masquerades as a live one.
  • Expiry: a plan that neither paid nor stopped within 4 × Outcome Horizon bars (clamped 50–4000) becomes STALE — the signal simply did not develop.

13.5 Management hints (break-even and chandelier trail)

With Show Break-Even & Trailing Hints on, a running plan grows two advisory levels:

  • BE STOP — a dotted line at entry, starting at the TP1-hit bar: “once TP1 paid, the managed stop of the remainder belongs at entry”.
  • TRAIL — a chandelier trail suggestion: the best closed source close since entry minus Trailing Stop (x ATR chandelier) × ATR (mirrored above price for shorts), never worse than the original stop. Refreshed on closed bars only (the drawing records its freshness watermark).

The plan tooltip carries the standard management note: scale out at TP1, move the stop to break-even, trail the rest.

13.6 Path transparency

The panel’s Path cell shows the plan’s MFE / MAE in plan-R units (e.g. +0.46R / -0.34R), so every HIT / VOID label on the chart is verifiable at a glance against how far price actually travelled.

14. RSI Momentum Trendlines

14.1 What they are

Two automatic lines in the oscillator pane, drawn through the RSI readings of the same prominence-filtered, confirmed pivots that feed the divergence engine:

  • Support — through the last two qualifying RSI low pivots; a confirmed source close below it (by more than Trendline Break Threshold RSI points) is a bearish momentum break.
  • Resistance — through the last two qualifying RSI high pivots; a confirmed close above it is a bullish break.

Qualifying anchors respect Trendline Min Pivot Gap (minimum bars between the two pivots) and Trendline Max Slope (RSI points per bar — a vertical line is not a trend, it is a spike). The engine searches up to six candidate anchor pairs, re-anchors only when a strictly better pair confirms, extends the line to the live bar every update, and detects breaks on closed source bars only — a break marker, once printed, is final. Breaks produce: an on-pane marker (Show Trendline Break Markers), an alert family (§11), two panel rows (TL Sup / TL Res with value and up/down/broken state) and ARP-PLAN-adjacent log lines when debugging.

Why momentum trendlines lead price A price trendline breaks when the market gives up a level; an RSI trendline breaks when the momentum behind the level gives up first. In practice the oscillator’s support line breaks several bars before the price chart’s own trendline in fading trends — treat it as an early-warning layer under your structure analysis, not as a standalone entry.

15. Session Markers

Thin vertical lines on the price chart at configurable UTC times — London and New York opens by default (07:00,13:30). Pure context: they never interact with signals, gates or statistics.

  • Marker Times (UTC, HH:MM list): comma / semicolon / space separated; invalid tokens are ignored; the parsed set is sorted so markers inside one wide bar emit chronologically.
  • Occurrence-complete: inside a bar wider than the marker interval (a Daily bar containing both 07:00 and 13:30, a Weekly bar containing five of each) every occurrence is emitted, each with its own composite key (bar, occurrence, minute) — no collision, no drop, up to the hard cap of 1500 objects.
  • Marker Window (chart bars): a rolling window of closed chart bars; older markers are trimmed.
  • Drawn on closed chart bars only; a transient draw failure heals on the next reload (no retry storm).

18.3 Debug Log: watching the gates work

Debug Log prints one line per rejected candidate — [Skip BullRegular] volatility spike (3.42x baseline) q=61.3 @ 2026-10-26 07:00 — and one per registration with its full metric set. When a chart “should have shown something and didn’t”, this is the answer key: the reason is always one of the named gates (§27), never a silent drop.

SECTION 2 — HOW TO USE

19. Installation & First Launch

  1. Import. In cTrader, open the Automate tab (or click the .algo / source file) and add AdvancedRSIPro.cs; build. The indicator appears under Indicators → Custom as AdvancedRSIPro. No permissions are requested (AccessRights.None).
  2. Attach. Double-click it on any chart. It opens in its own oscillator pane below price; the price chart receives only the plan levels, mirrored divergences (if enabled) and session markers.
  3. Pick a style first. Before touching anything else, choose Trading Style (§4). On a first install, Swing is the most representative baseline; Scalping for M1–M5; Position for D1+.
  4. Read the log. Open the log panel and read the profile dump (§18.1). Confirm the resolved source, bands and ladder look like the market you attached to.
  5. Let it warm up. The first pass replays history: pivots confirm, divergences register, plans draw, the statistics window fills. Nothing alerts during this pass — by design.
  6. Leave defaults for a week. The statistics and the learning buckets need live outcomes to become meaningful (§29). Judge the tool only after it has measured itself on your feed.

24. Trading Divergences

24.1 The anatomy of one signal

Read a divergence label left to right: BULL DIV A 65% = bullish, regular class, grade A, quality 65 %. Then verify with four glances:

  1. The legs. Price made a lower low while the RSI made a higher low (regular bull). The panel’s Legs cell quantifies both (e.g. 1.1A/13.4pts); legs barely above the minimums are structurally thin no matter what the score says.
  2. The grade. A = the swing between the pivots crossed the midline (a complete impulse); C = both pivots sit inside one excursion. Prefer A and B; treat C as context.
  3. The quality vs your distribution. Compare with the panel’s Avg qual for the window. A 65 in a feed whose average is 55 is a strong signal; a 65 in a feed whose average is 68 is ordinary.
  4. The plan. The drawn Entry/SL/TP1/TP2 is the coherent ruler for the idea. If the stop distance looks absurd for your risk budget, the signal is not for your account — the engine measures, you size.

24.2 Regular vs hidden in practice

  • Regular divergences are reversal patterns: they shine at the end of extended legs and fail in strong trends (the trend gate protects you from the worst of this — §27.2).
  • Hidden divergences are continuation patterns: they shine in established trends’ pullbacks. Their extra protection is the hidden MTF gate: a hidden bear printed into a strongly bullish higher-timeframe flow (≤ −33 aligned) is rejected outright.
  • Both share the quality machinery; your per-family win rates (panel buckets) will tell you which family actually works on your instrument — that is exactly what they are for.

25. Trading Crossovers

Crosses are the engine’s timing layer; divergences are its structure layer. The classic RSI playbook (“buy when RSI turns up out of oversold”) is exactly the OB/OS-exit family, made precise by the lambda hysteresis: with Signal Sensitivity Lambda = 0.92 the zone state clears only after RSI has moved 0.32 points (on 70/30 bands) back through the border — most one-tick whipsaws never print a marker.

  • OB/OS exit — the reversal timing signal; strongest when a divergence is recent and the MTF ladder is not opposed (the marker tooltip shows the bar’s confluence score).
  • Midline — the regime signal; useful as a filter (“only longs above 50”) more than as an entry.
  • Signal line — the momentum turn; the noisiest family, and the only one whose alert is off by default.
Crosses without context are coin flips In a strong trend, OB/OS exits fire repeatedly against the trend (that is what “trending” means for an oscillator). The engine does not hide this; it gives you the trend bias line, the Consensus cell and the MTF score so you can see the disagreement. Use crosses to time a direction you already have from structure or the ladder.

26. Multi-Timeframe Workflow

  1. Decide your primary. The chart timeframe is the decision timeframe; the source timeframe (if different) is the measurement timeframe. Most users keep them equal.
  2. Read the ladder top-down. The per-rung cells show each vote (e.g. h2 +51 BULL, h6 −66 BEAR). A ladder in agreement (|score| ≥ 33 with all rungs signed the same way) is the cleanest backdrop for any signal.
  3. Use the gate you actually want. SoftPenalty nudges quality; HardGate vetoes. If you cannot explain why a veto should exist on your market, stay on SoftPenalty.
  4. Trust the strict mapping. With the default strict mapping the ladder never shows you a higher timeframe’s in-progress bar as if it were knowledge. When you need the extra tick of freshness, switch it off consciously and accept that live display values can tick.
  5. Watch the flip alert. A BULL→BEAR ladder flip is a regime statement, not an entry: it tells you the backdrop under your open positions changed.

26.1 Case study: a coarser source (H8) on a finer chart

A real field configuration worth understanding: EURUSD with a custom H8 source on a finer chart (the panel reads TF h8 auto, source bars opening at 05:00 UTC while the chart grid runs 01:00 / 05:00 / 09:00…). Two lessons live in this one screenshot:

  • Marker placement (fixed in v3.24.5). Before 3.24.5 a closed-bar marker was drawn at the first chart bar of its source bar — with an H8 source, up to 8 hours (several chart bars) before the moment the cross could be known. Live, a BUY popped into the past at the start of the very bounce that confirmed it, so the bounce was already spent when the marker appeared and any subsequent decline read as “price fell right after the BUY”. Since v3.24.5 markers land on the last chart bar overlapping the confirmed source bar — the bar that was live at the knowledge point, adjacent to the trigger output. Identity charts never changed.
  • Context beats polarity. The same screenshot shows the engine’s own backdrop: Bias BEARISH, Consensus BEAR, weekly rung -73 BEAR. Every OB/OS-exit BUY in that regime is a counter-trend timing ping — crosses carry no trend filter by design (§25). The tool was not inverted; the backdrop said “do not take these longs”, in the panel, in colour.

27. Gates in Practice — Loosening and Tightening

27.1 The volatility gate family

Three gates answer “is this market tradable right now?”:

  • ATR gate (Adaptive by default): the engine models ATR% as a log-normal process (EWMA of log-volatility with an exact West correction) and takes your percentile of that distribution as the floor. Adaptive ATR Percentile = 20 means “reject signals in the quietest fifth of regimes”. Fixed uses your % directly (scaled by timeframe); Off removes the floor.
  • Volatility spike filter: rejects confirmations inside a >3× baseline ATR% shock — the news bar itself.
  • Falling-knife filter: rejects a bullish confirmation under a >4 ATR vertical drop (and the mirror for bears) within the last 2×Trend Slope Bars — no catching knives, no fading parachutes.

Loosen (lower percentile, disable spike/knife) only if Debug Log shows genuine signals dying in conditions you understand and accept. Tighten (higher percentile) if your journal shows losses concentrated in dead or shocked sessions.

27.2 The trend gate

The slope of the resolved trend EMA, in ATR per bar, decides how much counter-trend pain a signal may carry: hidden divergences are rejected when the trend runs against them beyond Max Counter-Trend Slope; regular divergences only at 3× that limit (“too strong to fade”). Between the limits the gate does not reject — it scores: alignment feeds 10 % of the quality score. The cyan bias line is this gate drawn, so you can watch it tighten and relax with the market.

27.3 The pattern gates

  • Cluster filter: no second same-direction signal within Min Pivot Distance bars and 1 ATR of price of a previous one — prevents pyramiding into one bottom at three qualities.
  • Follow-through (off by default): demands the confirmation close already inside the pivot→confirmation range in the signal’s direction. Turning it on makes the engine later and pickier — a legitimate scalping filter, a bad idea on slow timeframes.
  • Session filter: trades only inside market hours (symbol calendar in Auto, or your UTC window). The panel’s Session cell shows the current verdict.

28. Alerts Workflow

  1. Start minimal. Divergence + trendline break + plan levels (the defaults) cover structure, momentum shift and lifecycle. Add cross alerts only if you actually act on crosses.
  2. Set the cooldown from your bar rhythm. On M5 with a 20-bar cooldown you get at most one bullish ring per 100 minutes; on D1 the same number is a month. Cooldown is per direction, so it never masks the opposite side.
  3. Keep Only On Closed Source Bar on unless your execution style is explicitly intra-bar (Scalping preset). A ringing alert that can un-ring itself is worse than no alert.
  4. Route the log lines. With Write Alerts To Log on, every ring is also a text line — the audit trail that lets you reconcile alerts against markers and journal rows afterwards.

SECTION 3 — TIPS

31. The Golden Rules

  1. Pick the style before the parameters. The preset is 134 decisions made coherently; hand-tuning from Universal is rebuilding that coherence by hand.
  2. Read the resolved profile, not the dialog. The numbers that matter are in the start-up log and the panel’s config section — the dialog shows references.
  3. One truth per pane. The dotted bands, the zone gates, the MTF votes and the panel all read the same live bands. If something looks contradictory, one of your toggles changed a layer’s visibility, not its data (§37).
  4. Quality is a distribution, not a threshold. Calibrate against your own Avg qual; the factory 50/65/70 trio is a starting shape, not a law.
  5. Let the engine measure itself. Keep learning and the journal on until you have a reason to turn them off; the statistics are the only opinion in this product that is backed by your own data.
  6. Plans are rulers, not orders. Size positions from your account risk; the plan only makes the risk coherent and measurable.
  7. Never judge a configuration on fewer than ~100 resolved outcomes (§29).
  8. When in doubt, Debug Log. Every skipped candidate has a named reason; the engine never silently ignores anything.

33. What NOT to Do

  • Do not enable the regime-adaptive filter bank “because it exists”. It redefines the main line; every statistic you already collected describes a different oscillator.
  • Do not set Min Quality Score to 0 to “see everything”. You will see everything, including the candidates the engine proved worthless; your eyes will recalibrate to noise. Use Debug Log instead.
  • Do not mix rulers. The ATR ruler grades outcomes; the R ruler draws plans. Comparing a panel win rate against plan HIT counts is comparing two instruments.
  • Do not trade a STALE plan. STALE means the idea did not develop within four horizons — it is information, not an opportunity.
  • Do not stack two instances on the same chart with different styles and average their opinions; run them on separate charts and let the journals arbitrate.
  • Do not disable Strict Non-Repaint MTF Mapping to get earlier alerts — you would be buying freshness with look-ahead, the exact disease the engine is built to cure.

34. Performance & Chart Hygiene

  • The engine is incremental: every kernel updates only new or revised bars; the SMA carries a running-sum cache with periodic exact rebaselining. Normal tick load is a handful of bar updates.
  • Heavy operations (full replays) happen on start-up, on history reloads and on timeframe changes — expect a brief pause there, not during ticking.
  • Draw caps are real budgets: Max Drawn Divergences, Max Cross Markers, Max Visible Trade Plans, the session-marker window and the 1500-object marker cap. Raising all of them to maximum on a year of M1 history is the only way to make this product visibly slow — and it also makes the pane unreadable.
  • Panel refresh below ~100 ms with native controls buys nothing: the panel rebuilds only when dirty.
  • Keep Persist Learning on: restoring four buckets is cheaper than re-learning them, and the file is a few dozen bytes.

35. Verifying the Non-Repaint Claim Yourself

  1. Freeze a moment. On a demo chart, note the current bar time and take a screenshot of the oscillator pane and the price pane.
  2. Scroll away and back. Switch to another symbol and return (or detach/reattach the indicator). The history pass replays: every glyph that was on confirmed bars must reappear identically — same bars, same labels, same qualities.
  3. Bar-replay test. If your platform offers bar replay / playback, step through the bar where a divergence confirmed: the pattern must appear exactly at its confirmation bar, never earlier, and must not move afterwards.
  4. Journal cross-check. Copy the ARP-JOURNAL lines into a spreadsheet; the confirmation timestamps are the bars where glyphs appeared. A glyph whose bar is older than its journal line’s confirmation time would be a repaint — you will find none.
  5. Live-bar discipline. With Mark Only Closed Bars on, watch the forming bar: cross markers and divergence labels may preview nothing at all until close — that absence is the guarantee working. (Scalping’s live mode is the documented exception.)
